# Toyota Stadium

## Stadium
It's being renovated. So that's good, but the current iteration is clearly the relic of a bygone area. The Hunts clearly built the stadium with an intent of being able to do a little bit of everything, but nothing particularly good. The shade was limited to the recently-built Hall of Fame section, and the rest of the stadium was at the mercy of the brutal Texas sun. Not even any respite in the concourse area.

They force their own home supporters to suffer as well, moving them from the somewhat-cool beer garden behind the north goal to the southwest corner. Why, you may ask? To park some Toyota trucks and fireworks, which don't add much to the ambiance.

One can only hope that the new renovations live up to their promise, as the home fans deserve a lot more.

The stadium area is unremarkable. Downtown Frisco (or the vestiges of it) is fairly close with a few establishments, and a few restaurants dot the outer ring of the stadium's parking lots. Of which there are many.

## City
Normally, I'd go blast the fact that Frisco is so far that it should be considered its own city and ignore the wider Dallas-Fort Worth area, but that would be unfair. Frisco is 30 minutes from downtown Dallas, 45 minutes from downtown Fort Worth, and 40 minutes from the Arlington sports complex, but you're not going to stay in Frisco for the day.

If you do, there are several good breweries in Frisco and nearby McKinney, but you'll probably want to find a cool little neighborhood in Dallas proper with your car to enjoy what the Metroplex has to offer, like Deep Ellum, Bishop Arts, and Lower Greenville. 

There are plenty of museums, and the Sixth Floor Museum is a unique experience for history buffs. For the sports people, here's always some other sports to watch. Unless it's the playoffs, then you might be out of luck.

## Transit
It's bad. Real bad. It's somewhat usable in the central parts of Dallas, but DART does not serve Frisco. If you're flying in, just rent a car. There's no real way around it.

## Running
The White Rock Creek trail is a solid trail to park and run through. It's nestled in an accessible part of the city, though the Katy Trail is more iconic. The Trinity Levee trail is an option if you stay downtown but because it's in a floodplain, there isn't much cover.

## Overall
This is obviously biased given Austin FC's rivalry with FC Dallas (and the fact we basically lose every time we go there), but I wouldn't go out of my way to make this trip every year if we didn't take buses. Perhaps I haven't found my part of Dallas yet, but trips where you spend so much time in cars getting from place to place aren't my preferred way to spend vacation time.
